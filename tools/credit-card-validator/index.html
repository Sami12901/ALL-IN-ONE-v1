How Credit Card Validation Works

The Luhn Algorithm (MOD 10)

Created by IBM scientist Hans Peter Luhn in 1954, the Luhn formula validates numbers against accidental entry errors. Moving from right to left, every second digit is doubled. If doubling exceeds 9, the digits are summed (or 9 is subtracted). A number is valid if the sum of all digits is a multiple of 10.

IIN / BIN Ranges

The first 6 to 8 digits represent the Issuer Identification Number (IIN / BIN). They identify the card network and issuing bank: 4 for Visa, 51-55 & 22-27 for Mastercard, 34/37 for American Express, and 6011/65 for Discover.

Validation vs Authorization

Passing the Luhn check verifies mathematical authenticity and proper structure, but does not confirm whether an account is active or has available credit. Live verification requires a PCI-compliant payment gateway authorization.
